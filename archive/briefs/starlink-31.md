# Brief: Starlink-31

Angle: A correction read aloud. The catalogue's name and number for this entry belong to different satellites. The four satellites involved, put right, and what it is to be one of sixty identical things whose label can be wrong. Not warranted as a second full Starlink story: the first entry carries the phenomenon, this one carries the catalogue problem. (Companion entry: starlink-30.)

Form: A correction notice that becomes a confession, then a short exact ledger, then the decay physics, then a quiet return to the name tag.

Weights: history 30, technology 25, culture 15, reflection 30.

Entry: "A correction."

Technology to explain: Atmospheric drag at low altitude: thin air removes speed each orbit, a slower satellite sinks, lower air is thicker, so it speeds up. Plus the 2020 lowering of the batch.

Culture: The wrong name tag at a large gathering (tangent, not a claim about the satellite).

Humour: The narrator as the archive that made the mistake; "Gareth".

## Fact sheet

Identity (catalogue check)
- Catalogue id "starlink-31" carries NORAD 44239, which is STARLINK-25 (2019-029E), launched 24 May 2019, re-entered 1 Sep 2020 after 466 days, 227 kg, Starlink 0.9. [orbitalradar.com/satellite/44239; isstracker.pl/en/satelity/44239 gives decay 2020-09-01]
- The real STARLINK-31 is NORAD 44235 (2019-029A), re-entered 1 Oct 2020 after 496 days, 227 kg. [orbitalradar.com/satellite/44235]
- Starlink-30 is NORAD 44244 and Starlink-24 is NORAD 44238; see starlink-30 brief for dates (13 Oct 2020 and 24 Oct 2022, 508 and 1,249 days).
- All four are from the 24 May 2019 launch of sixty satellites (international designators 2019-029A, D, E, K).
- Starlink 25 was already decayed before 8 Sep 2020 [satobs.org]. Aerospace gives a prediction for Starlink 23 only as 2 Sep 2020; not used.

Decay
- 2020 sequence for the batch: lowered to about 443 x 445 km late May to June 2020; by Aug 2020 most at about 272 x 275 km, expected to decay within weeks. [satobs.org/seesat/Sep-2020/0055.html]
- Musk (April 2020): satellites launched without the later brightness mitigations would likely be retired and re-enter within three or four years. [Spaceflight Now 28 Apr 2020]
- Released together in a clump from one rocket, 60 satellites, each about 227 kg. [Spaceflight Now 24 May 2019]
- Atmospheric drag brings a failed satellite down within about five years at the lower orbit (SpaceX). [Spaceflight Now]

## Disputed or not to be used
- Starlink-30 reentry date 13 vs 14 Oct 2020.
- Why Starlink-24 stayed to 2022 is unsourced. How or when the catalogue mismatch arose is unknown; the story says only that the numbers and names do not match.
- Whether these four were among those lowered or raised normally is not stated by any source read.
- Blocked or unreadable: celestrak.org, in-the-sky.org, keeptrack.space.
