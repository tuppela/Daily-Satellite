# Brief: Landsat 8

Angle: A fifty-year diary whose latest volume had embarrassing early pages, and a thermal instrument that was wrong in public, got corrected, broke, and carried on.

Form: A diary. Open on 11 February 2013 (launch day, and the day Landsat 5 got its Guinness record). Treat the Landsat record as a diary kept since 1972. Explain the two instruments, then the thermal flaw as the early pages nobody wants to reread. Tangent: opening an old diary and flinching at the tone. The tangent returns with a short plain sentence, and the closing line comes back to the early pages.

Weights: technology 40, history 30, reflection 20, culture 10.

Entry: 11 February 2013, 10:02 Pacific time, Vandenberg, and the same day's Guinness news about Landsat 5.

Technology to explain: OLI (reflected sunlight, nine bands, 30 m, one 15 m band) and TIRS (heat, two bands, 100 m). Both look at a 185 km strip all at once and the orbit pushes it along like a broom. TIRS's three-position mirror (Earth, blackbody, deep space). Then the stray light: radiation from outside the field of view scattering onto the detectors.

Culture: Free data; the record kept whole by Landsat 5 after the loss of Landsat 6.

Humour: The old satellite gets the certificate, the new one gets the job. Owners advising caution on one of two bands. The NASA headline that says exorcism. A report that admits the root cause is not certain.

Reflection: The early pages of anything are the ones you can see trying.

Ending: Status in March 2026, design life versus age, then the early pages.

Dark comedy dose: 3

Length: about 830 words.

## Fact sheet

Launch and naming
- Launched 11 February 2013, 10:02 a.m. PST, from Vandenberg Air Force Base, California; to be renamed Landsat 8 after USGS took control in about 100 days; data then free to all users. [USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/february-11-2013-launch-ldcm]
- Atlas V rocket, launched on time, 1:02 pm EST; NASA built and launched it; USGS to operate. [SpacePolicyOnline: https://spacepolicyonline.com/?p=3395]
- Landsat 5 named Guinness "longest operating earth observation satellite", announced 11 February 2013; built to operate three years; ran 28 years and 10 months. [USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/february-11-2013-landsat-5-sets-guinness-world-record]

The record
- The first satellite, Earth Resources Technology Satellite (ERTS, later Landsat 1), launched 23 July 1972; NASA and USGS programme; NASA's article calls it the longest contiguous record of Earth's surface from space. [NASA: https://www.nasa.gov/earth-and-climate/landsat-legacy-nasa-usgs-program-observing-earth-from-space-turns-50/]
- First two Landsats had four spectral bands. Jim Irons (former director of Earth Sciences Division, NASA Goddard) on early colour-coded maps: people would get out coloured pencils or magic markers and colour in the different characters (paraphrase in the story). [same NASA article]
- Landsat 6 launched 5 October 1993 and did not reach orbit; Landsat 5's unexpectedly long life kept the data record going. [Space.com: https://www.space.com/19665-landsat.html; USGS FS 2005-3130: https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2005/3130/FS-2005-3130.pdf]
- Landsat 8 and 9 have a five-year design life that they may exceed. Programme more than 50 years old. [CRS: https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-06-16_IN12281_6f29be776d1e2fbebf9fded76e8d68a26d52767b.html]

Instruments and orbit
- Orbit 705 km, 99 minutes per orbit, swath 185 km, 16-day repeat; push-broom sensors. [USGS FS 2012-3072: https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2012/3072/fs2012-3072.pdf]
- OLI: nine bands, 30 m except a 15 m panchromatic band. TIRS: two thermal bands, 100 m. [USGS FS 2026-3001: https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2026/3001/fs20263001.pdf]
- TIRS scene select mirror: three positions (Earth imaging, onboard blackbody, deep space). [USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-8-thermal-infrared-sensor-scene-select-mechanism-anomaly]
- TIRS built at NASA Goddard; design, build and test about 3 years on an accelerated schedule; schedule pressure forced accepted risks. [ESA: https://earth.esa.int/eogateway/instruments/tirs/description; NASA NTRS: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20190001149/downloads/20190001149.pdf]

Stray light
- Out-of-field radiance scatters onto detectors; ghost level in band 11 about 8% or more, roughly double band 10. [Montanaro et al. 2014: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20150000753]
- 22 Aug 2013: discrepancies of 2 K or more against surface-water measurements. Oct 2013: band 10 advised as a single band. Jan 2014: band 11 not advised for quantitative work. 3 Feb 2014: reprocessing with average offset. Feb 2017: physics-based correction. [USGS calibration notices: https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/landsat-8-oli-and-tirs-calibration-notices]
- RIT scientists developed the algorithm; errors could reach 10 degrees C in extreme-temperature areas. [USGS newsletter Jan 2017: https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/volume-11-issue-1-january-2017]
- NASA article title: "Landsat 8 Thermal Data Ghost-Free After Stray Light Exorcism" (title seen in search results only; page not fetched). [https://landsat.gsfc.nasa.gov/article/landsat-8-thermal-data-ghost-free-after-stray-light-exorcism]

Mirror encoder anomaly
- Fall 2014 rising current in the encoder supply; yellow limit (FOT's threshold for powering off) reached December 2014; 19 Dec 2014 thermal processing suspended; 2 March 2015 switched to redundant side B; 7 March 2015 routine imaging; reprocessing completed 18 June 2015. Likeliest cause a resistive short; most viable cause conductive anodic filaments growing along fibres; root cause not certain. [USGS anomaly page above]

Status
- March 2026 USGS programme update: Landsat 8 "nominal operations", 750 new scenes per day, projected below 50% availability in 2030. [FGDC: https://www.fgdc.gov/ngac/meetings/march-2026/NLIPUpdateMarch2026]
- Landsat 7 decommissioned 4 June 2025 after 25 years; fuel and batteries depleted. [USGS: https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/end-era-landsat-7-decommissioned-after-25-years-earth-observation]
- Safehold 26 Jan 2023, later recovered (recovery page seen in search results only). [https://www.usgs.gov/landsat-missions/news/landsat-8-currently-safehold]

## Disputed or not to be used
- Catalogue desc "Fifty years of continuous Earth observation" describes the Landsat programme (since 1972), not Landsat 8 (launched 2013). "Environmental record" is loose: NASA says record of Earth's surface. Not repeated.
- Landsat 5 end date: SpacePolicyOnline says decommissioned January 2013; Space.com says until 5 June 2013. Not used.
- TIRS resolution: USGS FS 2012-3072 says both 100 m and 30 m; 100 m used (FS 2026-3001).
- TIRS design life three years (ESA) versus five-year satellite design life (CRS). Story says only the satellite's five years.
- Exact stray light cause element not identified in sources. Not stated.
- Rocket for launch (Atlas V) not used in story.
- Whether Landsat 8 is operating after March 2026 not verified by a later dated source.
- Pushbroom explanation ("pushed along like a broom") is general knowledge, not a sourced detail.
