# Brief: Tiangong

Angle: A window that cracked the day before the crew were due to go home, and how a space station's "permanence" is really a rota plus a spare capsule. The ordinary explanation (a leaf) against the real one (debris falling at orbital speed).

Form: Incident report in the first person, one case followed through in order: the discovery, the three-pane window, the swap of capsules, the emergency launch, the repair, the return. The station itself is explained in the middle. The leaf comes back at the end, changed.

Weights: history 45, technology 20, culture 20, reflection 15.

Entry: The final checks on the return capsule, the day before the planned return: the commander sees a triangular mark on the window and thinks of a leaf.

Technology to explain: What the station is (a T of three modules, Heavenly Palace), how it stays up (falling sideways around the Earth), how it is crewed (three at a time, six at handovers, a rota), and the window: an outer protective layer and two inner pressure-bearing layers.

Culture: The windscreen chip we all talk ourselves out of. Spares as things you pay for hoping to throw away. Offices that take longer than twenty days to reply.

Humour: The leaf in space. The arithmetic of one damaged lifeboat. Three firsts, "not arranged".

Reflection: "Permanent" means a rota. The unsaid paragraph: the crew's only vehicle was the one the agency would not trust. Keep respectful: no joke at the crew, who acted and reported.

Ending: The leaf was wrong about the speed, not the shape. Last report: another three aboard, two outside, one inside helping. Three again, still not arranged.

Dark comedy dose: 3

Length: about 800 words.

## Fact sheet

Status
- Latest dated source: China Manned Space Agency report via CGTN, published 28 Aug 2026: the Shenzhou-23 crew has been aboard since 25 May 2026, experiments "progressing as planned"; two crew did a spacewalk of about 5.5 hours to repair a solar wing and install space debris protection, the third supported from inside [CGTN 2026-08-28]. On 22 June 2026 CMSA announced an expansion plan to add a module, making the T a cross [cislunarspace.cn 2026-06-23]. I found no source later than 28 Aug 2026.
- Tianhe core module launched 29 April 2021, 03:23:15 UTC, Long March 5B from Wenchang [Wikipedia Tianhe core module].
- Station is T-shaped from Tianhe, Wentian (launched 24 Jul 2022) and Mengtian (launched 31 Oct 2022) [Planetary Society; Wikipedia mirror]. "Tiangong" means Heavenly Palace [AP via NPR 2026-05-25; Universe Magazine].
- Typical crew three, up to six at handovers; permanently crewed since 5 June 2022 [Wikipedia mirror, as of 25 Apr 2024].
- Orbit (April 2024 figures): 386.4 by 391.8 km, 41.47 degrees, period about 92.3 minutes [Wikipedia mirror, as of 25 Apr 2024].

The window incident
- Shenzhou-20 launched 24 April 2025 with Chen Dong, Chen Zhongrui, Wang Jie; return was planned for 5 Nov 2025 [SpacePolicyOnline; Space.com].
- Chen Dong spotted the anomaly during final checks one day before the planned return; he told CCTV he "spotted something like a triangular on the viewport" [Space.com via Yahoo]. His first thought was whether a small leaf had stuck to the outside of the window; he realised it could not have, because they were in space [Live Science]. They photographed the damage and sent it to the ground [China in Space].
- Wang Jie: the viewport has an outer protective layer and two inner pressure-bearing layers; the crew were "safe as long as the cabin pressure doesn't change" [Space.com via Yahoo].
- Chen Dong's initial judgement: the crack was in the outermost layer, had penetrated it, and would not affect their continued stay on orbit [China in Space].
- CMSA said the capsule "does not meet the requirements for the astronauts' safe return" [Space.com via Xinhua/CMSA]. Its 14 Nov analysis: crack most likely caused by an external impact from space debris [Universe Today].
- The crew left on 13 Nov (landing 14 Nov 2025, Dongfeng, Inner Mongolia) in the Shenzhou-21 spacecraft, nine days late; Shenzhou-20 stayed docked [Space.com; SpacePolicyOnline].
- Six people were aboard for about nine days [NASASpaceflight]. The Shenzhou-21 crew's only emergency vehicle was the damaged Shenzhou-20 until Shenzhou-22 arrived [NASASpaceflight].
- China keeps a Long March 2F and a Shenzhou on standby at Jiuquan during crewed missions [Space.com, citing SpaceNews' Andrew Jones].
- Shenzhou-22, planned for spring 2026, launched uncrewed 25 Nov 2025 (04:11 UTC) with supplies and a window crack treatment; it docked about 3.5 hours after launch [NASASpaceflight; SpacePolicyOnline].
- Chen Zhongrui: from discovering the problem to the Shenzhou-22 launch and docking "only about 20 days passed" [China in Space].
- CMSA described three firsts for the Shenzhou-21 crew: a return postponed by debris impact, a return on a different spacecraft, and an emergency spacecraft launch [CGTN 2026-05-29].
- The Shenzhou-21 crew did three spacewalks, inspected the window and installed debris protection [CGTN 2026-05-29]. The empty Shenzhou-20 landed 19 Jan 2026; CMSA reported its exterior generally intact [Space.com via Yahoo; SpacePolicyOnline].
- The Shenzhou-21 crew landed 29 May 2026 at 8:11 pm Beijing time after 210 days, in good health [CGTN 2026-05-29].

Disputed or not to be used
- Catalogue agency "CNSA": the station is run by the China Manned Space Agency (CMSA). Correct it.
- NORAD 48274 is the Tianhe core module ("CSS (TIANHE)"), not the whole station.
- Sources disagree on which capsule carried the Shenzhou-21 crew home; the sourced account is that they returned in Shenzhou-22 [CGTN]. Story does not name it.
- Shenzhou-21 arrival date (31 Oct vs 1 Nov 2025): not used.
- Mass (about 100,000 kg), design life and Wolf Amendment/ISS exclusion: sources vary or do not attribute cause; not used.
- Shenzhou-24 (Pakistani astronaut) planned Oct 2026: unconfirmed, not used.
- Whether the crack was discovered before or after 4 Nov handover varies; story uses "the day before the planned return".
