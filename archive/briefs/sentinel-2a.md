# Brief: Sentinel-2A

Angle: A satellite built for seven years and given fuel for twelve, replaced on paper in January 2025 and kept on anyway. Nobody loved it into staying; the sums came out slightly in its favour. At the end of its career it was asked to work nights, to teach its successor's job.

Form: A leaving speech that cannot be finished, because the leaver keeps being asked to stay. Open on the number seven. Move through the postponements in order (replacement, petition, "not retire yet", the night shift) and end on the retirement fuel it has carried all along.

Weights: technology 30, history 30, culture 15, reflection 25.

Entry: The plan. "Seven years."

Technology to explain: The pushbroom. A row of light sensors lies across the satellite's path, covering a strip of ground 290 km wide at once; as the satellite moves, the row records line after line and the picture builds up from the motion, like a broom pushed along a floor. Stripe filters over the detectors pick the 13 bands, some invisible to the eye, so different bands meet the same ground at slightly different moments (up to about 2.6 seconds apart).

Culture (light): Finland. SYKE (the Finnish Environment Institute) uses Sentinel-2 imagery in its Tarkka service to watch lakes for algae and to help warn the public.

Humour: The seven-year plan and twelve-year fuel; the rocky first week; "yet"; a daylight camera asked to work nights; being told you did "very well" at something you were not built for.

Reflection: Most of us are kept for being useful on the numbers, not for being loved. The unsaid paragraph sits alone. Tangent: the family photograph taken in the same place every year (everyday human experience, not a claim about the satellite).

Ending: Return to "yet": it carries the fuel for its own retirement and nobody has asked it to use it. Last line: "Not yet."

Dark comedy dose: 3 (several dark turns, admissions about the narrator's reactions, one tangent, one callback on "yet" and on the fuel)

Length: about 840 words.

## Fact sheet

Mission and status
- Sentinel-2A launched 23 June 2015 on a Vega rocket from Kourou, French Guiana. [ESA Facts and figures, esa.int]
- Sentinel-2B launched 7 March 2017; Sentinel-2C launched 5 September 2024, also on Vega. [ESA Facts and figures; European Spaceflight 1 Mar 2025]
- Sentinel-2C replaced 2A in operation on 21 January 2025. [Sentiwiki s2-mission]
- The Sentinel-2A extension campaign began in March 2025 and, in an announcement dated 15 May 2026, was "prolonged until the end of 2026", i.e. until 31 December 2026. [Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem node 3911, 15 May 2026; Sentinel Online notice, 30 April 2026]
- ESA/Copernicus (Feb 2025): the Commission kept 2A running after user groups asked for continued access; the satellite was in good health. Christoph Kautz, Director for Satellite Navigation and Earth Observation at DG DEFIS, said: "Sentinel-2A will not retire yet." [European Commission DG DEFIS, 6 Feb 2025]
- From early 2025 2A was repositioned (January to March 2025) to a position 36 degrees from 2B, to complement the acquisition plan. [European Commission DG DEFIS, 6 Feb 2025; Sentiwiki]
- No source I found says it has been switched off; the extension notice dated 15 May 2026 is the latest dated status found. Whether it is still operating after that date is not confirmed.

Design life and fuel
- Design lifetime: minimum of seven years [ESA Facts and figures]; 7.25 years, with batteries and propellant provided for 12 years of operations [Sentiwiki s2-mission].
- Propellant for up to 12 years of operation, including de-orbiting manoeuvres after retirement. [European Spaceflight, 1 Mar 2025]
- Mass 1140 kg including 123 kg of fuel. [ESA Facts and figures]
- I could not find any source saying why 12 years of fuel and batteries were provided.

First days
- During the Launch and Early Orbit Phase, teams dealt with a sticky valve, a star tracker problem and a GPS unit issue; all were resolved and the satellite was in excellent health. First thruster manoeuvre "went exactly as planned". [ESA/phys.org, 26 June 2015]

Instrument (MSI)
- Multispectral imager with 13 bands spanning 443 nm to 2190 nm; 290 km swath; resolutions 10 m (four bands), 20 m (six bands), 60 m (three bands). [ESA Facts and figures]
- Push-broom concept: each detector row images a line of ground as the satellite moves. Stripe filters on top of the detectors select the individual bands. A dichroic beam splitter divides visible/near-infrared from shortwave infrared. [ESA Sentinel Online MSI instrument page]
- Each band observes the same ground point at a slightly different time, with offsets from about 0.03 s to about 2.6 s. [ESA Sentinel Online MSI instrument page]
- Orbit: sun-synchronous, 786 km altitude. Each satellite revisits a location every 10 days; two satellites 180 degrees apart give a 5-day revisit at the Equator. [ESA Facts and figures; Sentiwiki]
- First civil optical mission to acquire three red-edge bands, used for vegetation analysis. [Wikipedia summary; not used]
- The MSI relies on reflected sunlight and is normally switched off over night-side passes. [ESA, "Sentinel-2 explores night vision", 15 Jan 2026]

Petition and extension
- A petition titled "Copernicus should keep Sentinel-2A operational after Sentinel-2C launch" was published 09/03/2024 (before 2C launched in September 2024 on either reading of the date format). It asked Copernicus to extend 2A operations "for as long as possible", said the programme planned to stop 2A once 2C was operational for cost reasons, and argued that clouds cover much of the land so a third satellite would improve revisit frequency and the quality of maps. It had 1,686 signatures at time of capture (goal 1,500). A January 2025 update said it had been successful. [pytition.ethibox.fr]
- European Spaceflight reports 1,683 signatures. [European Spaceflight, 1 Mar 2025]

Night campaign
- Night-time imaging campaign started 3 December 2025 and spanned one full Sentinel-2A cycle, covering selected gas flares, bright lights, wildfires and fishing activity. [Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem node 2874]
- Detected targets included gas flares from oil production in the Middle East (images from Qatar and Iraq; Iraq on 12 December 2025), a wildfire in India, and fishing boats off South Korea (9 December 2025). [ESA 15 Jan 2026; Pixalytics]
- Ferran Gascon, Sentinel-2 Mission Manager: "Switching on the satellite to image at night required a great deal of energy, but Sentinel-2A did very well." [ESA 15 Jan 2026]
- ESA says the experiment put strain on the satellite but it remains in remarkably good health. The test is to help pave the way for Sentinel-2 Next Generation, which is intended to image at night over certain regions. [ESA 15 Jan 2026]

Finland
- SYKE (Finnish Environment Institute) uses Sentinel-2 data through its Tarkka service to monitor lake water quality, with a focus on algal blooms; Tarkka regularly monitors most lakes larger than one hectare. ELY Centres and SYKE use the images to warn the public through local newspapers and websites so residents avoid bloom-affected lakes. [ESA Sentinel Online success story, 29 Nov 2022]
- Results are shared through the open Tarkka web map service. [SYKE VESISEN project page]
- Note: these sources say "Sentinel-2", not 2A specifically.

Data policy
- ESA Member States approved a full and open Sentinel data policy, with no-cost access, reported 27 November 2009. [esa.int]

## Disputed or not to be used

- Petition signatures: 1,686 (petition page) versus 1,683 (European Spaceflight). Story says "more than sixteen hundred".
- Launch time: 01:52 GMT on 23 June 2015 (phys.org); not used.
- Petition's claim that clouds cover about 70 per cent of land on average is the petitioners' figure; not used.
- The petition's cost-reason claim about why the programme planned to stop 2A is the petitioners' statement; not used.
- Catalogue description: "every five days" is the two-satellite constellation figure (2A with 2B originally, now 2B with 2C); one satellite alone revisits every 10 days. "Entire planet" is wrong: systematic coverage is land and coastal areas between 84 degrees N and 84 degrees S. "Free" is supported by the Sentinel full and open data policy.
- Sentiwiki says 2A is "nearing the end of its operational life"; ESA says the night test came "while it nears the end of its operational life"; neither gives an end date.
- Wikipedia calls 2A "now operational" in a summary of uncertain date; not used.
- Status after 15 May 2026 is unconfirmed.
