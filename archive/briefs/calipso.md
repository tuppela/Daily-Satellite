# Brief: CALIPSO

Angle: A lidar that lit up what was always in the air, and a mission that ended the way it measured: by sending a signal and listening for an answer.

Form: A single image examined from several sides: the torch beam in a dusty room. Explain the instrument through it, walk down the atmosphere from smoke to dust, then the machine's own long ending, and return to the torch.

Weights: technology 35, history 25, culture 15, reflection 25.

Entry: A flash of laser light sent down about twenty times every second, and the wait for what comes back.

Technology to explain: A pulse of light goes down; the time the echo takes tells the height of what scattered it, the strength tells how much there was. Stacked, this gives a vertical slice of air. Image: a torch beam in a dim room lights the dust already there. Beam 70 m wide on the ground, 1 m telescope.

Culture (light): The biscuit tin nobody refills in view: a supply you never notice until it stops (invented everyday tangent, not a claim about the satellite).

Humour: NASA counted the dust in semi trucks, to the last truck. The team deciding which parts of the day to ignore. Narrator's relief at not having to check ten billion measurements.

Reflection: What keeps us going is delivered by something unthanked; the dust did not need to be looked at.

Ending: Back to the torch: nobody sends the flash now, the dust was there before.

Dark comedy dose: 3

Length: about 850 words.

## Fact sheet

Mission and status
- Joint NASA (Langley) and CNES mission, launched 28 April 2006 with CloudSat. [https://www.nasa.gov/missions/calipso/first-long-duration-lidar-satellite-mission-calipso-ends]
- Designed for 3 years, delivered measurements for 17. [https://science.nasa.gov/mission/CALIPSO/ ; https://asdc.larc.nasa.gov/news/official-end-of-calipso-science-mission]
- Science mission ended 1 August 2023 by agreement of NASA and CNES; fuel reserves exhausted, and in its decaying orbit it could no longer generate enough power for the science instruments. [asdc.larc.nasa.gov news posted 2 Aug 2023; CNES 3 Aug 2023]
- More than 10 billion lidar measurements; more than 4,000 publications (CNES). [NASA feature, Sept 2023; CNES]
- CNES continued operations for some months (platform technology experiments, making the propulsion system passive). Final passivation week of 11 December 2023; final command at dawn on Friday 15 December, from CNES control centre in Toulouse, sent symbolically by Ron Verhappen of NASA/LaRC, who had worked on the mission for 25 years. [https://cnes.fr/actualites/extinction-satellite-calipso, 26 Dec 2023]
- PROTEUS passivation: monitoring off, solar panels turned away from the Sun, batteries discharge until voltage is too low to run the platform; no signal received is the proof. [same CNES page]
- Principal investigator Dave Winker said (KJZZ, 23 Oct 2023) gravity would bring it down over roughly 24 years, it would "basically burn up and break up into tiny pieces", nothing significant expected to reach the ground. [https://www.kjzz.org/2023-10-23/content-1860928-qaz-after-orbiting-earth-17-years-what-will-happen-calipso-satellite] (A third-party reading, not an agency re-entry prediction.)
- Orbitalradar.com showed it at 662 to 663 km, TLE epoch 2026-10-29, remaining life estimated 10 to 25 years. [https://orbitalradar.com/satellite/29108, read 31 Oct 2026] (Its "Active" label is not reliable and is not used.)
- Final data: 32 products; NASA ASDC will host all CALIPSO data and documentation; project website to be decommissioned (update 13 May 2026). [https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/data/alerts-outages/calipso-final-data-products]
- Partner CloudSat (radar) left the A-Train 22 Feb 2018 after losing a reaction wheel; CALIPSO left 13 Sept 2018 (705 km to 688 km) to join it in the "C-Train"; CloudSat radar operations ceased 20 Dec 2023. [https://science.nasa.gov/mission/a-train/a-train-news] (Kept out of the story apart from one mention.)

Instrument (CALIOP)
- Two-wavelength polarisation lidar, 532 and 1064 nm. [NASA mission page]
- Pulse rate about 20 Hz (20.16 Hz in Tackett et al. 2025; 20.25 Hz in the payload user guide: use "about twenty").
- 1 m diameter telescope; beam diameter 70 m at the surface; footprints every 333 m along the ground; first light 7 June 2006. [Winker et al., NTRS 20070035884]
- Pointed 0.3 degrees off nadir to avoid strong mirror-like returns from still water. [same] (Left out of the story for length.)
- Two identical lasers; the first had a slow leak and was expected to degrade within the 3-year mission; switch to the backup in 2009 (March per Tackett 2025, February per Wikipedia: story says only "2009"). Backup ran nearly flawlessly until low-energy pulses appeared from mid-2016, from arcing in the canister as pressure dropped; team developed procedures that reject affected data in small segments. [https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/18/6211/2025/]

Results
- Australia 2020 wildfires: smoke between 9 and 12 miles above the surface. [NASA feature, Sept 2023]
- Iceland 2010 ash observations used by Volcanic Ash Advisory Centers to alert aviators. [same] (Cut from story.)
- Saharan dust (Yu et al., GRL, published 24 Feb 2015; NASA release 22 Feb 2015): 2007 to 2013 data; average 182 million tons a year past 15W; 132 million tons still airborne near South America; 27.7 million tons settle over the Amazon basin (NASA: 104,908 semi trucks); about 22,000 tons of phosphorus a year, roughly what the Amazon loses to rain and flooding; source an ancient lake bed (Bodele Depression) in Chad. [https://www.nasa.gov/missions/calipso/nasa-satellite-reveals-how-much-saharan-dust-feeds-amazons-plants ; https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/11775]

## Disputed or not to be used
- Catalogue says "smoke and aerosols" and "fertilises the Amazon": supported (Yu et al. 2015), but "mapped" is loose; it estimated transport over 2007 to 2013.
- Laser switch month: Feb vs March 2009. Pulse rate 20.16 vs 20.25 Hz. Altitude 705 km (to 2018), 688 km after, Wikipedia 701 to 703 km: story says "around seven hundred".
- End of instrument operations 30 June 2023 (Earthdata) vs science end 1 Aug 2023 (NASA/CNES): story uses 1 August.
- Re-entry date: no agency date found. The 24 years is Winker's, by KJZZ; do not state a year. Third-party tracker lifetime estimates not used.
- EarthCARE as successor: not confirmed as a hand-off by any source read; not used.
- Gizmodo says "NASA announced it on a Tuesday"; Wikipedia "20 years" mission duration: not used.
