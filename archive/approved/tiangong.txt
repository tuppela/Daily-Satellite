The day before they were due to go home, Chen Dong was running the final checks on the return capsule when he noticed a triangular mark on its window. His first thought, he said afterwards, was that a small leaf had stuck to the outside.

He dismissed it quickly. They were in space.

I'd like to defend the leaf. It is the right first thought. The ordinary explanation is the one we all reach for, and it stays in charge for exactly as long as the evidence lets it.

So, the window. As Wang Jie described it, there is an outer protective layer and two inner layers that carry the pressure. Three layers. The outer one takes whatever the sky throws at it, and the inner two keep the air in. The crack, Chen Dong said, was in the outermost layer, and had gone right through it. He judged that it would not affect their staying on board. The China Manned Space Agency judged that the capsule no longer met the requirements for a safe return.

You have done this with a windscreen. A chip appears. On Tuesday it is a bit of dirt. On Wednesday you tilt your head and decide it is a bit of dirt with a shape. By Thursday it has been there so long that it belongs to the car, and you tell yourself it has settled, that things settle. The chip hears none of this. It has a crack's patience and a crack's plans.

Chen Dong, for the record, did the other thing. They photographed it and sent the pictures down to the ground.

I would have tilted my head and gone to bed. I say that with no pride at all.

The station is called Tiangong, which means Heavenly Palace, and it is laid out as a T of three modules: Tianhe, Wentian and Mengtian. It stays up the way anything up there stays up, by falling. The last figures I found, from April 2024, put it around 390 kilometres up, moving sideways so fast that the ground keeps curving away beneath it, going round about every ninety-two minutes and never arriving.

It is meant to hold three people at a time. The idea is that the next three arrive before the last three go, so for a few days there are six, and the place stays occupied.

That is what permanent means, up there and down here. A rota.

On 14 November 2025 the crew who should have left on the 5th went home, nine days late, in the capsule that had carried up their replacements. The damaged one stayed docked. That left three people aboard whose only crew vehicle was the one the agency had said it would not trust to bring anyone down.

I find that a difficult sentence to sit near. Not because it is dramatic. Because it is arithmetic.

The programme, as one reporter puts it, keeps a rocket and a spare capsule on standby at Jiuquan while a crew is up. A spare is something you pay for in the hope of throwing it away. This one flew. Shenzhou-22 had been meant for spring 2026, as the next crew's ride, and instead it went up on 25 November with nobody aboard, carrying supplies and a treatment for the cracked window. It docked about three and a half hours after launch. Only about twenty days passed between discovering the problem and that launch. Twenty days is less than most of us need to get an answer from an office we have written to.

The agency counted three firsts for the crew who stayed behind: a return postponed by debris, a ride home in a capsule other than the one they came up in, and an emergency launch. I promise I did not arrange that.

They carried out three spacewalks, to inspect the window and to fit debris protection to the station. The cracked capsule was treated, and it came home empty on 19 January 2026. The agency reported its outside generally intact. The crew themselves landed on 29 May 2026, after 210 days, all in good health.

As for the leaf. It was ruled out because they were in space, and I have come round to thinking that is backwards. The agency's analysis put the damage down, most likely, to an impact from space debris, and space is the one place where something drifting past can crack a window, because it is falling too, on its own track, at a speed no leaf has ever managed. The ordinary explanation was not wrong about the shape. It was wrong about the speed.

The last report I found, dated 28 August 2026, has another three aboard, there since 25 May. Two went outside to repair a solar wing and fit more debris protection. The third stayed in, helping.

Three again. Still not arranged.
